# Originality Rules

The workflow is reusable; the visual result is not a reusable template.

## Brand Fingerprint

Before storyboarding, define all seven dimensions:

1. Composition grammar: split screen, editorial spread, modular grid, cinematic stage, data HUD, collage, or another system.
2. Type personality: serif authority, grotesk energy, technical mono, handwritten warmth, or a justified combination.
3. Graphic vocabulary: category-specific objects, diagrams, textures, strokes, cards, icons, or illustration forms.
4. Image/subject treatment: define how supplied media participates, or explicitly define the typography-to-illustration relationship for text-only work.
5. Color logic: background/foreground roles and one deliberate accent behavior.
6. Transition family: wipes, masks, page turns, hard cuts, shutters, folds, scans, or another coherent family.
7. Rhythm behavior: impact-heavy, progressive reveal, elastic, percussive, restrained, or another music-linked cadence.

## Anti-Template Test

A new brand must differ from any example on at least five of seven fingerprint dimensions. Merely changing copy, portrait, colors, or icons does not pass.

Reject a direction when:

- the same scene order could accept another brand with only text replacement
- the dominant motif has no connection to the category
- all beats use the same entrance animation
- supplied media is decorative rather than part of the identity story
- copy is obscured to simulate energy
- the final lockup resembles a demonstration brand

## Controlled Reuse

Reusable elements may include validation scripts, data schemas, safe-area helpers, audio-map parsing, rendering commands, and generic animation utilities. Do not bundle or reuse demonstration portraits, copy, logos, palettes, compositions, timing maps, or visual assets.

## Three-Direction Exercise

Generate three short internal directions:

- one category-native direction
- one personality-native direction
- one audience-native direction

Select the direction with the clearest brand recognition, strongest music fit, and lowest similarity to prior work.

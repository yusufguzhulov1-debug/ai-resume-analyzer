# Story map

**Best for:** the narrative backbone of a product, sliced into releases.

## Layout

- **The backbone runs across the top**, in the order a user does them, not in
  the order the team will build them. Those two orders differ, and the gap is
  the argument the map makes.
- **Stories hang below each backbone step in priority order**, highest first.
- **The cut line is the only editorial mark on the picture**, so it gets the
  accent: a dashed accent rule across the whole frame with `RELEASE 1` in the
  mono just above it.
- Everything above the line is `backend`; everything below is `optional`. The
  fade is the release decision rendered as a treatment.

## Budget

Five backbone activities, three slices, twelve cards.

## The reveal

1. The backbone, 6 frames apart.
2. The cards, fast (4 frames), filling the columns.
3. **The cut line last**, drawing across the frame over about 24 frames.

That order is the whole shot: here is everything we could build, here is the
line, here is what ships. Revealing the line first throws the argument away.

## Anti-patterns

- Ordering the backbone by priority. It is a narrative; order it by the story.
- More than one cut line. Two releases is two shots.
- Cards above the line that nobody has committed to. The line is a promise.

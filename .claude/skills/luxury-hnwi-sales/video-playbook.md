# Video Playbook — Reels & Scripts in the Luxury Register

How to apply the Luxury Academy principles when writing scripts for the
avatar skills ([`viral-video-script`](../viral-video-script/SKILL.md),
[`avatar-reel-composer`](../avatar-reel-composer/SKILL.md),
[`avatar-talking-video`](../avatar-talking-video/SKILL.md),
[`cuento-reel`](../cuento-reel/SKILL.md)) or any short-form video about premium
sales, client psychology, or high-ticket positioning.

## Golden rule

**One lesson per reel.** The choice paradox applies to content: a reel that
teaches three things teaches nothing. Pick one principle from
[principles.md](principles.md) and build the whole script around it.

## The channel-proven script arc

Every Luxury Academy video follows this arc — it works because the structure
itself models the psychology it teaches:

1. **Hook (0–5s)** — a contrarian reframe or status-threat observation.
   Never "5 tips for…". See hook formulas below.
2. **Mechanism (5–20s)** — name the psychological concept in plain terms
   (price-quality bias, emotional contagion, trust signaling, reactance…).
   Naming the concept is the credibility beat: specificity signals expertise.
3. **Story (20–45s)** — one concrete anecdote with texture: the private
   banker who said "you don't need that fund", the therapist who tripled her
   rate, the consultant who looked like he was asking permission to be in
   the room. Real details (a place, a number, a quoted line) beat abstractions.
4. **Practice (45–70s)** — exactly 2–3 actionable steps. What to say, what
   to stop saying. Include a literal example phrase the viewer can steal.
5. **Aphorism closer (last 5s)** — one quotable line that compresses the
   lesson. No summary, no "so those were the tips".

## Hook formulas (extracted from the channel)

- **Contrarian inversion**: "Why [common advice] makes you look [opposite of
  intended effect]" — *"Why playing hard to get makes you look desperate"*,
  *"Why talking too much makes you look weak"*.
- **Pre-verbal judgment**: "People decide X about you before you [speak / finish
  your first sentence]" — *"The reason people doubt you before you say a word"*.
- **The forbidden secret**: "I probably shouldn't be teaching you this…" /
  "The trick that works better than any pitch deck".
- **Named villain**: "The one mistake that's ruining your [brand/pricing/
  meetings] — and you're making it right now."
- **Confession + observation**: "I paid £14 for an average sandwich — and
  caught myself justifying it. Here's what that teaches about pricing."
- **Status test**: "When a wealthy client goes silent after you quote your
  price, they're not thinking about the number. They're watching you."

## Example aphorism closers (steal the register, write new ones)

- "Tell someone not to spend their money, and they'll spend it with you forever."
- "Real confidence isn't a performance at all. It's the absence of one."
- "The price judges you, not the product."
- "People don't remember what you do. They remember how you make them feel."
- "Real scarcity comes from having something worth protecting in the first place."

## Tone and delivery rules

The delivery must model the lesson — a hyped-up reel about calm authority
destroys itself:

- **Calm authority**: measured, unhurried, slightly amused. Think expert at
  a dinner party, never street pitchman.
- **No hype vocabulary**: ban "amazing", "insane", "game-changer",
  exclamation marks, ALL CAPS. Over-enthusiasm reads as insecurity (see
  KAvpvkMmcro in principles.md).
- **Certainty of language**: no hedging ("maybe", "I think", "kind of").
  Lead with the conclusion, then the reasoning.
- **Pauses are content**: write beats of silence after the strongest lines.
  For avatar/TTS delivery, insert explicit pauses (punctuation, break tags,
  or scene cuts) after the hook and before the closer.
- **Specificity**: named concepts, real numbers (£300/h, 40 pieces a year,
  5-second pause), quoted dialogue. Vague claims invite scrolling; precise
  details hold attention.
- **Dry wit is on-brand**: one understated humorous aside per script max
  ("a barista called Minty with a trust fund").

## CTA style

Understated, exclusive, never begging:

- Good: "If this is the level you operate at, the full framework is on the
  site." / "Follow if you sell to people who can't be sold to."
- Bad: "SMASH that like button!", "Link in bio NOW", countdown urgency,
  "don't miss out!".

## Avatar production notes

- Script length for a 30–60s reel: 80–160 words. The arc above compresses:
  hook (1 sentence), mechanism (1–2), story (2–3), practice (2), closer (1).
- Voice direction for TTS/voice-clone: lower pitch end of range, slow rate,
  minimal energy spikes; warmth through timing, not volume.
- Avatar wardrobe/setting: apply counter-signaling — plain well-fitted
  clothing, no visible logos, uncluttered premium environment (see
  RTWlRghLS60). Muted palette; black/white/navy read as luxury.
- B-roll: quiet luxury imagery (craftsmanship close-ups, architecture,
  understated interiors) — never money-flexing clichés (cash stacks,
  lambos, champagne spray). Those signal the opposite audience.
- Subtitles: minimal, elegant type; no rainbow karaoke captions.
- Music: restrained, low-tempo, no EDM drops
  ([`bg-music`](../bg-music/SKILL.md) / [`bg-music-hq`](../bg-music-hq/SKILL.md):
  ask for "understated, elegant, minimal" moods).

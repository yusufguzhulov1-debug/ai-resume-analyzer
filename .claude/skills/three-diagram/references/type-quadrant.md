# Quadrant

**Best for:** two axes of judgement, and where things land between them.

## Layout

- **The quadrant names are the argument; the dots are evidence for it.** Name
  all four, even the one nothing sits in, because an empty quadrant is a finding
  too.
- **One quadrant gets a 5% accent wash** — the one the video is arguing for.
  That wash is the editorial move, and it should arrive before the dots so the
  dots look like they landed in it.
- **Axis names at the ends**, in the mono: `IMPACT` up the left, `EFFORT` along
  the bottom. Not on the crosshair.
- **The focal item is bigger, outlined, accented.** One of them.
- The crosshair is two 18% ink rules, not an axis with ticks. A quadrant has no
  scale and pretending otherwise invites arguments about coordinates.

## Budget

Twelve items. Six reads better, and six is usually all the evidence there is.

## The reveal

Crosshair, axis names, quadrant names, then the wash, then the dots 6 frames
apart, then the item names. The wash before the dots is the whole rhetorical
order.

## Anti-patterns

- Numbered axes. If the numbers are real, this is a scatter plot.
- Items on the crosshair, which reads as an unwillingness to decide.
- Four accent colours, one per quadrant.

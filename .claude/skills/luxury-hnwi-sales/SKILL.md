# Luxury & HNWI Sales Psychology

Knowledge distilled from the Luxury Academy YouTube channel (Paul Russell,
consumer behavior psychologist, trainer of luxury brands and private banks).
This file holds the core rules and workflows. Load the reference files only
when needed:

- [principles.md](principles.md) — full knowledge base by theme, with source video attribution.
- [video-playbook.md](video-playbook.md) — hook formulas, script arcs, and delivery rules for reels/videos.
- [web-playbook.md](web-playbook.md) — page anatomy, copy vocabulary, and design cues for premium sites/landings.

## The 12 core rules

1. **Sell desire and identity, never need.** Nobody needs a Rolex. Luxury
   purchases are identity markers — symbols of achievement, status, and
   belonging. "Needs analysis" language kills luxury sales.
2. **Emotion decides, reason ratifies.** Lead with story, exclusivity, and
   feeling (limbic system); only afterwards reassure the rational brain with
   value framing, social proof, and risk reduction.
3. **Never justify price.** Justifying admits the price needs defending.
   State it and let the client decide what it means. Price-quality bias means
   the number itself constructs the perceived value.
4. **Never discount.** Discounting luxury erodes the brand permanently and
   attracts price-motivated customers. Create exclusivity instead: limited
   editions, private access, curated experiences.
5. **Only real scarcity.** Countdown timers and fake urgency trigger
   persuasion knowledge and reactance — affluent buyers spot them instantly
   and start evaluating you instead of the product. Real scarcity is capacity,
   structure, and standards.
6. **Restraint builds trust (trust signaling).** "You don't need the more
   expensive option" — acting against your short-term gain locks in loyalty.
   Saying no with authority beats saying yes with enthusiasm.
7. **Status is read before content.** Composure, pace, silence, certainty of
   language, and congruent appearance are processed before anything you say.
   Hedging ("does that make sense?", "I might be wrong but") destroys
   authority; specificity and pauses hold it.
8. **Silence is authority.** Answer, then stop. Rushing to fill a pause reads
   as seeking approval. HNWIs use silence as a status test — don't flinch.
9. **Fewer options, more conversion.** Cognitive overload freezes decisions.
   Present 2–3 options as a framed comparison, sequence information broad to
   specific, pause and recap.
10. **Personalize by wealth type.** Inherited wealth wants heritage and
    discretion; self-made wants bespoke innovation that celebrates their
    success; earned wants craftsmanship and professional prestige; unexpected
    wealth wants guidance and simplicity. One approach does not fit all.
11. **Speak the language of luxury.** price → investment; buy/sell →
    acquire/own; contract → agreement; deposit → initial investment;
    objection → area of concern. Never call yourself "luxury" — real luxury
    brands never do. Be an expert, not a salesperson.
12. **Experience over symbols.** Marble, monograms, and macarons are empty
    symbols. Memorable luxury comes from noticing, anticipation, and the
    unexpected gesture. People remember how you made them feel.

## Workflow: video/reel mode

1. Pick exactly ONE lesson per reel (rule 9 applies to content too).
2. Open with a contrarian or curiosity hook (formulas in video-playbook.md).
3. Follow the channel-proven arc: hook → mechanism (name the psychological
   concept) → story/example → 2–3 practical steps → one-line aphorism closer.
4. Tone: calm authority. No hype, no exclamation marks, no "amazing/insane".
   Slow pace, deliberate pauses. The delivery itself must model the lesson.
5. Understated CTA, if any.

## Workflow: web/landing mode

1. Identify the audience wealth type/tier and pick the matching angle
   (heritage / bespoke / craftsmanship / guidance).
2. Follow the premium page anatomy in web-playbook.md: authority hero,
   specific proof, 2–3 framed offers, stated (not justified) pricing,
   discreet social proof, single unhurried CTA.
3. Apply the vocabulary substitutions and the counter-signaling design cues
   (restraint, whitespace, no badge clutter, no urgency widgets).

## Anti-patterns (never do)

- Countdown timers, "only 3 left!" on digital goods, fake urgency of any kind.
- Discounts, coupon codes, "affordable luxury" (it does not exist).
- Exclamation-heavy, over-enthusiastic copy — over-performance reads as
  insecurity and kills trust.
- Self-labeling as "luxury"/"premium" instead of demonstrating it.
- Feature dumps, long option lists, walls of specs (cognitive overload).
- Justifying or defending the price anywhere in copy or script.
- Begging language: "just checking in", "bumping this up", multiple follow-ups.

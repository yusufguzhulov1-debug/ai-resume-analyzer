# Timeline

**Best for:** events positioned in time, where the gaps are part of the story.

## Layout

- **Position by date, not evenly.** A timeline whose events are equally spaced
  is a list with a line through it, and the gaps are usually the most
  interesting thing on it.
- **Alternate above and below the axis** so labels never collide, with a stem
  from the axis to each label.
- **The final event is the accent**, with a larger dot. A timeline is almost
  always an argument that ends somewhere.
- **Two lines per event**: the label in the sans, the value in the mono
  (`18 min`, `64 s`). The second line is what turns a chronology into a
  measurement.
- Period markers along the axis in the mono (`M0`, `M6`).

## Budget

Five or six events. More and the stems start colliding at a readable label size.

## The reveal

1. The axis drawing across, 26 frames.
2. Period markers.
3. Then per event: stem, dot, label, value, 9 frames apart.

Letting the axis draw first and the events land on it is the shot; revealing
them together makes the axis look like a decoration.

## Anti-patterns

- Evenly spaced events on a real date axis.
- Labels on one side only, which forces them to shrink.
- A timeline for a process with no dates. That is a process diagram.

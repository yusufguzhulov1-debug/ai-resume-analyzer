# Wardley map

**Best for:** a value chain against evolution: what to build, what to buy, and
what is moving.

## Layout

- **Y is visibility to the user** (the anchor at the top), **X is how evolved
  the component is**. Both axes mean something specific; neither is a score.
- **X ticks are the four stages**, named in the mono: `GENESIS`, `CUSTOM`,
  `PRODUCT`, `COMMODITY`. Never numbers.
- **The chain is thin 22% ink links**, not arrows. A value chain is a
  dependency, not a flow.
- **The anchor is larger**: the user need everything hangs from.
- **The focal component is the one about to move**, accented and outlined.

## The movement arrow

The only prediction on the map, so there are two at most and they are the
accent: a 4px accent line from the component's current position to where it is
heading, labelled `COMMODITISING` in the mono just above. Everything else on the
map is observation; this is the claim, and it should look like one.

## Budget

Nine components, twelve links, two movement arrows.

## The reveal

Axis, the y-axis name, the chain, the dots, the component names, then the
movement arrow last with a slow stagger. Observation first, claim last.

## Anti-patterns

- A grid of numbered cells. Evolution is not a score.
- Movement arrows on half the components, which is a wish list.
- Leaving the axes unnamed because the shape "looks like a Wardley map".

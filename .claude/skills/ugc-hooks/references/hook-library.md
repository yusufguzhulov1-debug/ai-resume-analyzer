# Hook library

Sixty-plus openings, grouped by family. Each row is the **line** and the **visual** it needs. Temperature is who it works on: cold (does not know the problem is solvable), warm (knows the problem, not the product), hot (knows the product).

Swap every specific for the user's own. The detail is the whole point, and a borrowed detail is a generic one. Every number in these lines is illustrative: a social-proof, authority or result line ships only with the user's real figure.

Read it when you are choosing the line for a hook family you have already picked, or writing variants B and C.

---

## Pain

Names the failure state, flatly. Never performed, never bright. The tone is someone tired of it, not someone selling the cure.

| Line | Visual | Temp |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Three hours. For fifteen seconds of video." | A timeline scrubber covered in clips, hand on a trackpad | Cold |
| "I have opened this app forty times this week and never finished anything." | A cluttered editor, cursor idle | Cold |
| "This is the part nobody warns you about." | A render progress bar at 11 percent | Cold |
| "Every launch. Same panic." | A calendar with tomorrow circled | Warm |
| "I paid someone four hundred dollars for this." | A mediocre video playing small | Warm |
| "My designer is asleep and we ship in the morning." | A Slack thread with no replies | Warm |
| "Still exporting." | A spinner, held one beat too long | Cold |
| "The video is always the last thing and always the thing that's late." | A checklist, one item unticked | Warm |

## Curiosity gap

Opens a loop the viewer has to close. The line must not be answerable from the frame. If the visual gives it away, the gap is shut.

| Line | Visual | Temp |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Nobody told me you could do this in a browser." | A half-built thing, deliberately ambiguous | Cold |
| "I found the setting that fixes it." | A settings pane, the row blurred | Cold |
| "This is not a template." | Something that looks exactly like a template | Cold |
| "Wait for the part at eleven seconds." | A timer overlay starting | Cold |
| "I was doing this wrong for two years." | Hands mid-undo | Cold |
| "There is a reason this one feels different." | Two videos side by side, both playing | Warm |
| "Everyone's videos look the same now. Here's why." | A grid of near-identical thumbnails | Cold |
| "One thing changed and the whole thing worked." | A single line of text being edited | Warm |

## Pattern interrupt

Breaks the expectation in the first half-second. The visual does most of the work; the line can be one word.

| Line | Visual | Temp |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Stop." | A hand shutting a laptop, hard | Cold |
| "Don't make this." | A polished corporate video, cut off mid-swell | Cold |
| "Delete it." | A file dragged to the trash | Cold |
| (silence) | A phone dropped onto a desk, caption lands a beat later | Cold |
| "Wrong." | A red cross slammed over a screenshot | Cold |
| "Okay this is actually insane." | Mid-reaction, already leaning in | Cold |
| "No. Like this." | A cursor undoing something, fast | Warm |
| "Put the phone down for one second." | Extreme close, off-centre | Cold |

## Social proof

Borrows somebody else's judgement. Only ever with a real number the user gave you. A fabricated proof hook is the worst thing in this document.

| Line | Visual | Temp |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Four hundred people made one of these last week." | A wall of thumbnails, scrolling | Warm |
| "My whole team switched in a day." | A shared workspace with several cursors | Warm |
| "Three people asked me what I used for this." | A DM thread, the question visible | Warm |
| "This is the one everyone's been sending me." | A link preview card | Warm |
| "It hit the front page and I still don't know why." | A ranking board | Warm |
| "Replying to the comment I keep getting." | A pinned comment card | Hot |
| "Our support inbox is just people asking how." | An inbox with repeated subject lines | Warm |

## Contrarian

Attacks what the viewer assumes. The least predictable family in the library: it lands hard or not at all. Always test it against a safer family, never ship it alone.

| Line | Visual | Temp |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Stop paying an editor." | An invoice | Warm |
| "Templates are the reason your ads don't work." | A template gallery | Cold |
| "You don't need a camera." | A camera, pushed out of frame | Cold |
| "Most launch videos are a waste of money." | A slick video playing muted | Cold |
| "Do not do what the tutorials tell you." | A tutorial paused | Cold |
| "This tool is worse than the one you have. Here's why I use it." | Two icons, one crossed out | Warm |
| "I'm not going to tell you it's easy." | A blank editor | Warm |

## Authority

Positions the speaker as someone who would know. Needs a real credential from the user. Without one, cut the family.

| Line | Visual | Temp |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "I've shipped forty launch videos. Here's what changed." | A contact sheet of past work | Warm |
| "I edit for a living and I stopped opening After Effects." | A dock, one app quitting | Warm |
| "Six years doing this. First time I've seen it done in a browser." | A browser window, full screen | Warm |
| "We ran this across two hundred ads." | A results table | Warm |
| "Here's what the agencies charge for exactly this." | A rate card | Warm |

## Result

Leads with the outcome, backwards. Works when the outcome is visually obvious in one frame.

| Line | Visual | Temp |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "This took eleven minutes." | The finished video playing | Warm |
| "One prompt. That's the whole thing." | The prompt, then the result | Warm |
| "From a link to this." | A URL, then the video | Warm |
| "Zero to shipped before the coffee went cold." | A cup, a finished export | Hot |
| "Here's the before. Here's twenty seconds later." | Split screen, both states | Warm |
| "I didn't touch a timeline once." | A finished video, no editor in sight | Hot |
| "Same script. Nine versions." | A grid of nine variants | Hot |

## Direct callout

Names the viewer. Only for hot traffic; on a cold feed it reads as an ad immediately, because only ads talk to you like that.

| Line | Visual | Temp |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "If you're launching this week, this one's for you." | A calendar, a date circled | Hot |
| "Founders who hate making video: watch this." | Direct to camera, close | Hot |
| "You already have everything you need for this." | The viewer's likely toolset, laid out | Hot |
| "This is for the person who has a demo and no video." | A screen recording, unedited | Hot |
| "Indie devs, stop scrolling." | Something visually abrupt | Hot |

## Combinations worth trying

The strongest hooks are usually two families stacked, verbal from one and visual from the other.

| Stack | Why it works |
| --- | --- |
| Pattern interrupt visual + pain line | The frame stops the scroll, the line explains why they should care |
| Result visual + curiosity line | They see the outcome, the line withholds the method |
| Pain visual + contrarian line | The frame agrees with them, the line disagrees |
| Social proof visual + direct callout line | Warmest combination in the list, for retargeting only |

## What never works

- A logo, a wordmark, or a brand colour field as frame zero.
- "Hi, I'm ___ and today I'm going to show you..."
- A question the viewer does not care about ("Tired of wasting time?").
- A held static frame with text fading in.
- Any line that needs the next line to mean something.
- A number nobody gave you.

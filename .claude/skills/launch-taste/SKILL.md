# Launch taste: choose what the film is, so it feels intentional

A median launch film fades up a headline, pastes the landing page underneath, puts a glow in the corner and ends on a logo. A premium one says one sentence, makes it visible with the product's own material, holds back everywhere except at one moment, and lets sound arrive exactly where the picture changes. The difference is a handful of choices made before any scene is built. This skill is those choices.

It sits between `direction` (the process: proposition, idea, family, curve, beat table) and the owner skill (the format's structure: `launch-playbook`, `announce-feature`, `brand-sting`, `announce-milestone`, `app-store-preview`, `freeform-video`). Use it to answer direction's Step 3 (the idea) and Step 4 (the style family) for any launch-type film, and to judge the cut at the end. `motion-language` and `sound-design` still own every number for moves and mix; this skill only says which of them to reach for and why. Frames are at 30 fps.

> **These are references, not rules.** The numbers, beat sheets, style families and examples here are starting points distilled from work that landed well, not a recipe to fill in. The user's instructions always win over anything in this skill. When a different idea serves the brief better, propose it and try it: change the structure, break a default, invent a device, and write what you changed and why in `VIDEO.md`. What stays fixed is correctness: determinism, legibility and safe zones, loudness and clipping, licences and credits, never putting words in a real person's mouth, and the sound bans in `sound-design` (no whoosh; never a synthesised noise bed, room tone or "air", which reads as wind or hiss on phones). The checks at the end are a quality bar to clear, not a template to reproduce.

Where these principles come from: two studies, plus GenMotion's own templates. The first was twenty motion-design launch, feature, milestone and brand films (design-engineer launches, open-source tools and showcase pieces; no live action), graded strictly. The second, and **the taste this skill aims at**, is a curated collection of twenty-two social-native launch films posted on X by product teams (AI builders, design tools, browsers, consumer fintech and trading apps, a parody): mostly 8–30 s, real UI made cinematic, type cards interleaved with product, glossy objects, light on edges. Where the two disagreed, the second won unless the point was correctness (legibility, rights, determinism). Moves were learned only from the films that worked; the weak ones supplied the anti-patterns. The references mark which rules several films agree on and which rest on one precise example.

## When to use

- Any launch-type film, alongside its owner: a product or app launch, a feature announcement, a brand sting, a milestone, a store preview, a freeform promo for a product or company.
- "Make this launch video feel premium", "what style should our launch film be", "our launch video looks generic", "it has no idea", "the sound feels pasted on".
- Before writing direction's Idea and Style family lines, and again at the self-critique.

Not for: edited footage of people (`video-editing`), UGC ad formats (the `ugc-*` owners), or a teaching explainer (`explainer`, though §Sound to picture still applies). It never replaces the owner: the owner's beat sheet, lengths and checks win for its format.

## Seven beliefs

1. **One sentence, made visible.** If a stranger cannot say it back after one muted view, the film is a list.
2. **The film must need time.** Ask what a still image would lose. A continuity, a convergence, a count becoming a quantity, a sound arriving on a pixel: good. "Nothing": the concept is missing, and polish will not fix it.
3. **The material is the product's own**: its nouns, units, glyphs, output and UI. Decoration (glows, blobs, particles, corner metadata) is what a film uses when it has no idea.
4. **Restraint only reads against contrast.** Stillness is confidence when one thing is clearly alive; stillness everywhere is absence.
5. **Shape beats speed.** A quiet start, a build, one peak at the biggest contrast, a calm resolve.
6. **Sound is placed by meaning**: on the event, as long as the event, rising for "yes", gone for the payoff.
7. **Direction is subtraction.** Most fixes are removals.

## The decision path

Make these in order; each narrows the next. Write the answers into `VIDEO.md` (see "Write it down").

### 1. The one thing: feel, or understand?

From the proposition, pick which the viewer leaves with. **Understand** ("it turns a URL into a spec") points to proof: the product working, a visible before and after. **Feel** ("your work follows you", "this is serious engineering", "you did a lot this year") points to a small story, an object, or a world. Both is a list; pick the lead and let the other ride along.

**Feel-led films need a someone.** A trace of the person the feeling belongs to (a hand, a name in their own writing, their desk, their street) or of the outcome object (the thing saved for, the trip booked, the shelf finally stocked) is on screen by the second beat, because a feeling with nobody in the frame reads as a gauge.

**A claim about effort or physics is shown, not written.** "By hand", "one tap", "no tools", "in seconds", "magnetic": the picture proves it. Show the hand or fingertip doing the one action, a count of the elapsed seconds beside it, the snap or seat that only a magnet makes. If the claim lives only in a caption, the film reads as automatic, or as any rival's.

Then write the still-image test in one line: "As a still, this would lose ___." If the blank is empty, go to step 2 before anything else.

### 2. The concept device

Pick the device before the look. These patterns recur in films that work; `references/concept-devices.md` has each with when to use it, fresh examples, how to build it and how it goes wrong.

| Device | Reach for it when |
| --- | --- |
| The product makes the film | It produces visuals, type, layout or motion; proof beats claims |
| The invisible made felt | The benefit is "nothing went wrong" (sync, offline, backup, privacy): a small story with an interruption makes the viewer need it |
| One fixed sentence, swapped notation | One promise to several audiences |
| Many → one | Consolidation, integration, "one place" |
| Output as material | The product generates content: build the mark or the hero object from it |
| The unit becomes the quantity | A milestone or a scale claim |
| The journey | Data over time, a recap, a route |
| The edit of old → new | A rename, a replacement, a companion format |
| The timeline drives the input | The product is itself motion or a parameter |
| Two worlds, one crossing | Positioning against an incumbent idea |
| The brand becomes the UI | Logo and product must arrive in one breath |
| Change the medium, not the subject | The honest subject is a cliché (a globe, a galaxy, a network) |
| Fixed silhouette, changing skin | Versatility |
| Ranked countdown | Several results of different weight |
| The prompt is the headline | The product is driven by typed input (an agent, a builder, search): the request, set at headline size, is the copy |
| The control, lifted | One control is the feature (a field, a toggle, a bet button): lift it out of the UI to poster scale and let it morph or act |
| The rebus line | A sentence whose key noun is its glyph (an icon, a coin, a token stack, a photo) sitting inline at cap height |
| A tap releases the many | A small action unlocks abundance: the toggle throws coins, the click spills tokens |
| Reveal, then redact | Privacy: show the exposure in a familiar place, then replay it masked |
| Throw out the old tools | Replacing a category of tools: generic stand-in icons, physically discarded |
| A history under one sentence | Weight and lineage: fast macro shots of the old way under one line that builds |

Sketch three (from the product's own visual world, from the feeling, from an unexpected format) and keep the least obvious one that passes direction's swap test. A device that needs the voiceover (or a caption) to explain it is the wrong device.

Three rules every device obeys:

- **When the product's noun is the category's stock image** (a shield or padlock for security, a rocket for "launch", a cloud for storage, a waveform for audio or voice, a sparkle or magic wand for AI, a chat bubble for messaging or assistants, a glowing brain for "intelligence"), the noun alone fails the swap test: lead with the product-specific noun (the doc you owed, the shipment that was stuck) rather than the category's, and at the device's completion pair it with the person or the outcome only this product's user gets.
- **When the whole category demos the same way, the stock demo fails the swap test too.** Prompt → a generic sign-up screen assembling (every prompt-to-UI tool), an amount typed → Pay → a tick (every payments app), a dashboard filling (every analytics tool): cover the logo and it is any rival's film. Tie every beat to the product's own differentiator and the user's own words: generated layers named with the prompt's phrases ("plant photo", "one button"), not "Frame / Title / Button"; a wallet's "self-custody" visible inside a proof beat (a key glyph, the address, a "From your wallet" header on the pay card). The brief's category noun or differentiator (self-custody, open-source, on-device, editable) appears in a proof beat, not only on the end card.
- **The device persists through every proof beat, and does something in each.** After the reveal the proof is built *from* the device, not beside it, and the device acts in every run (fills, refuses, converts, counts), never sits as a static frame (a held headline beside a static UI is a still); a contrast device (two worlds, before and after, a refused crossing) keeps both sides in frame while the product works, the antagonist inert or empty. "Many" means many of the device's own event, never a wall of generic cards, tiles or dashboard panels.
- **Each run says something new.** A run's line adds one fact the brief supports (what goes in, where it runs, what comes back, how long it takes); a paraphrase of the proposition is padding, and a run with nothing new to say carries no line.
- **The 10 goes on the half only this product has.** When the device's first half is the category's (any competitor's film could show the parcels being routed, the files syncing, the dashboard filling), its completion is not the memorable moment: the part only this product does is, even when it is a subtraction (many → none: the field emptying, the queue at zero, the last copy deleted). Build the densest state as the run-up and land the 10 on that difference, not on the resolve after it.

### 3. The style family

Choose the look that best carries the device, not the one that looks most "premium" in the abstract. The taste family says *why*; direction's house family letter supplies the numbers (palette, type, timings). `references/style-map.md` has each family's motion and sound personality and its classic failure; read it before writing the Palette, Type and Motion lines.

| Taste family | Says | Suits | Fails for | House family |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Kinetic editorial type | A point of view, wit | Manifestos, renames, multi-audience, teasers, a film's first 3–4 s | Anything that must be seen working; type alone past ~20 s | A, or B's type on a calm ground |
| UI-led story film | "It fits your day" | Consumer and productivity software, invisible features | Infra without UI, hype | B (D for one world) |
| Maker's proof film | "It's real, run it" | Dev tools, libraries, generators, the product making its own film | Consumer emotion, products that can't show output | B |
| Product-as-instrument | Precision, depth | Models, APIs, infra, data, security | Warmth, "it's easy", visual tools | G (HUD) on dark; on a light ground (paper, white) D or B's numbers |
| 3D object hero | Tangible value, craft | Consumer, fintech, devices, stings, generative output on an object | Workflow products, more than one idea | C |
| Data story / journey | Achievement, earned scale | Recaps, milestones, onboarding, "everything we shipped" | One feature; under ~15 s | J, or D |
| Playful collage | Curiosity, versatility | Creative tools, identity reveals, social loops | Precise or technical brands | G (paper) |
| Dark cinematic minimal | Drama, focus | Model launches, idents, hero loops behind type | Bright feeds, friendly consumer tone | A ground, not a family: keep A, C or G's numbers on it |
| Terminal / retro | Builder credibility | CLIs, open source where the command is the CTA | Mainstream audiences | G (HUD) or A |
| Component sizzle | "Parts that behave" | UI kits, SDKs | Outcome products | B on dark, or A |
| Title-card interleave | "It's live, here's why it matters", fast | Feature and product launches for X, LinkedIn and feeds, 8–30 s, with 2–5 crisp facts and some UI | Vague claims (the cards become clichés); films that need one long run | K |
| Prompt-led demo | "Ask, and it's built" | AI builders, agents, search, anything driven by a request | Products with no input box | B (K's cards between runs) |
| Perspective UI showcase | "Pro tool, serious money" | Trading, finance, dashboards, pro creative tools whose UI is the asset | UI that is dense at feed size and can't be magnified | K or B on a dark ground, C's light for rims |
| Glossy icons and tokens | "Everything you use, here", play | Integrations, tokens, apps, consumer fintech, many → one | Calm-trust enterprise, health | C (soft gloss) |
| Gradient-light mood | Craft, warmth, possibility | Creative and AI tools for non-technical buyers; output-as-material | Proof-first launches; dense UI | C ground on B's UI, or I |
| Editorial documentary | Lineage, conviction, a thesis | Company and platform manifestos, money with history, community drops | Single features; any film without rights to its footage or photos | G (paper or newsprint) |

Defaults when nothing else decides: an app with screens → UI-led story or maker's proof (feed cut: title-card interleave); a typed-input product → prompt-led demo; no UI at all → instrument (serious) or kinetic type (opinionated); one number → data or 3D object; only a logo → 3D object or collage; tokens, coins or integrations → glossy icons and tokens. The owner's own default family still wins ties.

### 4. Motion personality

Set one physics from the feeling, then reuse it everywhere (`motion-language` holds the numbers):

- **Calm trust** (finance, health, enterprise, luxury): no overshoot, long ease-outs, longer holds, locked or slow camera.
- **Confident premium** (most launches): snappy in, slow settle, no bounce; a thrown object caught softly; the camera eased at both ends.
- **Playful** (creator, consumer, community): overshoot 6–10% on one role only (a button, a sticker, a stamp).
- **Technical** (instrument, terminal): stepped and digital moves plus exactly one organic counter-motion (a wave, a breath), so the world feels alive.

Then decide **what is allowed to move**: one focal element per moment, everything else settled. And decide **the rhythm**: busy passages cut fast (0.5–0.9 s), resolutions hold long (4–5 s); give the hero beat about twice the time and minor beats half. Ten beats of the same length read as a slideshow.

### 5. Sound role

Name what leads (`sound-design` decides levels and sources):

- **Music leads**: a hype film, a sting, a recap. Lock arrivals to the grid, then **cut or thin the music on the payoff** and let a tail carry the hold.
- **The product's own sound leads** (a voice tool, an audio app, anything that clicks or plays): the bed leaves when the product speaks, and stays out.
- **Voice leads**: cut in the speaker's breaths, keep a bed under it, never let a pause become a blank picture.
- **Picture leads** (muted placements): every claim lives in the frames; visualise sounds as echoes of their source's shape; sound is a reward for unmuting.

`references/sound-picture.md` has the sync rules (latency, length, pitch direction, removing the bed, anchoring the lift, tails).

### 6. The restraint budget

Write these four lines; they are what makes the film look decided:

1. **One accent**, and what it means (the moving part, the active state, the payoff word, the CTA). Nothing else gets it. It must still read in the film's widest shot and at thumbnail size: a 0.17-unit orange stem was correct by the rules and invisible on every frame (judged), so colour the whole part that carries the meaning (the housing, not a stem) and capture the widest frame to check.
2. **One rule broken, at the climax**: the one physical shot in a flat film, the one serif word in a sans film, the one time the whole ground takes a hue, the one brightest frame. Spend it once. A colour field may *follow* the device's completion, never replace it (step 7). If the broken rule is the camera (the film's only move), every other beat, the hero beat included, moves the subject instead of the camera.
3. **One transition grammar**: a signature carried by something in the frame, a workhorse, hard cuts only on the music (direction Step 7).
4. **One silence**: where sound drops away on purpose (before the reveal, under the payoff, after the hero number).

### 7. Peak placement

This table is the one place peak placement is decided; `launch-playbook`, `direction` and the other owners defer to it. Put the peak where the film's kind says, and build it from the device, never from a transition:

| Kind of film | Peak | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reveal-led launch, milestone (the name or the product *is* the news) | 25–33% | The reveal early; everything after is evidence and resolve |
| Accumulation or outcome (a count, a fill, a build that completes; a story with a turn) | 60–75% | The device needs its units, or the story its setup, before it can complete |
| The product's sound or output is the payoff | 85–95% | The picture calms while the product takes over |
| Sting | entrance in the first 10%, lock at 40–60% | Grab the first second, land the mark, decay |
| Hype / instrument teaser | 70–100%, with the picture simplifying as the sound climbs | Contrast between dense sound and calm picture makes the lock inevitable |

**The two-peak shape** (an accumulation or outcome film that must still name itself early, as most launches must): the name reveal at 25–33% is a *secondary* peak (energy ≤ 7, no breath needed, no flood), and it names what the viewer has already been watching; the device's completion at 60–75% is the one 10, with the breath before it. Write both frames in the Energy curve line, and say which is the 10. Never two 10s. The name lands **on or beside the device while it keeps acting** (docked beside it, read off its header, written into its own UI); a mark and name on an empty ground mid-film, with nothing of the device in frame, is a logo card, and the film gets one logo card, at the end.

Every peak, whichever kind:

- **No peak on a flat flood.** Neither the name reveal nor the payoff line lands on a bare colour field: the device completes on screen and holds ≥ 30f before any flood, and the line sits on or beside the completed device. A word on a flat colour could close any competitor's film.
- **It reads with sound off.** The biggest picture change in the film lands on the hit frame, and the words change on it too (a word swapped, a counter snapping to its new state), so a muted viewer sees the turn without the cue. A line that lands on a hit is fully legible **on** the hit frame: a blur-up, glow-resolve or per-word stagger finishes before it (pre-roll its start by the reveal's length; a judged peak had its last word still blurred on the hit), and a line that arrives 0.5 s early and fills the breath has missed the hit too.
- **In a feed cut of ≤ 20–25 s the claim is stated by 1–2 s.** A completion peak at 60–75% may pay the claim off, but it must not be its first statement: state it on a 2–4 word card or in the product's own words (a header, a readout already on screen) by frame 30–60, and keep any setup (typing an amount, a loading state, a phone rising) to ≤ 1 s (30f). A wallet film whose "under a second" first appeared at 61% (9.8 s), after 2.7 s of typing, read as setup to the scroll-past viewer (judged).
- **Repeats tighten.** Before the peak, a calmer breath (direction's 10–30f); after it, slow down so the result has weight. Repeats that build toward it tighten (for example swaps of 6, then 4, then 2 frames) with slight irregularity; identical spacing reads as a timer. A count device puts the thing being counted against (the battery, the deadline, the budget) on screen from frame 0. Slowness belongs to the last unit before the peak, not the first.
- **The opening is fast, whatever the shape.** The device (or its first unit) is on screen and acting by frame 60. The first *run* of units (everything before the tightening) takes ≤ 2.5–3 s (75–90f), and between two units nothing visible stops for more than 1 s (30f): overlap the next unit's start into the last one's close. When a unit is a thin mark (a tick, a dot, a stroke, a small glyph), show the first one large (≥ 15% of the frame width) or make each unit's change cover ≥ 2% of the frame; a 100 px mark changing on a 1920 frame reads as a stall. **A duration claim** ("ready in 30 seconds", "set up in a minute") is shown compressed: the wait takes ≤ 75f of film, in visible steps or a time-skip (a readout jumping 0 → 6 → 18 → 30), never at a near-real rate; the number is the proof, not the wait.
- **A camera move that carries the peak is under way on the hit.** Its ease-in starts 10–14f before the hit frame, so the hit shows the picture changing; a push ends with the subject in the centre third at ≥ 40% of frame height (never grown in place at the edge); a pull-back used as the broken rule scales about the focal element so the focal stays fixed while the world arrives; labels land after the move, and nothing in the moving world crosses a visible word (`three-camera`, Moves that carry the peak).

## The social launch cut (8–30 s, posted under a message)

Most launch films now play in a feed under a post that already carries the details. The film carries the feeling and one or two facts, fast. What the films that worked share [several]:

- **Interleave cards and proof.** A type card of 2–4 words held by the hold formula `max(30, 9 × words + 15)` from legible (33f for two words, 51f for four), then a product beat (45–90f), then a card. Cards are the voice, product beats the proof; never two cards in a row without a reason (a setup/payoff split is the reason). The cut between them is carried by something in frame: one brand shape that opens and closes (an arc, a lens, a band of light), the panel rising through the words while they blur out, a defocus-through. A different wipe per cut is still a wipe zoo. **The cards are full-frame** (or the card the product rises through), interleaved with the product beats: a caption column beside one held shot of the product for the whole film is not the interleave, and two judged films lost their rhythm that way. Full-frame means the type fills the frame (cap height 15–25% of the frame height, one accent or bleed word, `three-type`), not a small word centred on black. **In a band film** (`device-mockup`'s message band over a persistent device), 1–2 full-bleed cards plus the band carrying the copy satisfy the interleave.
- **A cover lands on content.** At most **4 background-only frames per turn** (a flat ground, no word, no product): the next card's first word is on screen the frame the cover closes (or resolves inside the closing iris), and the next shot opens out of the card's last legible frame. Never stack a full cover and an exit-then-cut gap on one turn: the two empties add up (a judged film showed 6–8 black frames at each of five turns, 1.2 s of nothing in 20 s, which read as dropped frames). Carry the cover's own shape onto the card (a ring shrunk to a rim) so the carrier exists on both sides (`three-transitions`).
- **One motif carries the film.** The best each own one look that survives every beat, transitions included: glowing rims on a dark ground, a brand gradient framing every prompt, one flat saturated field, paper in daylight, monochrome newsprint with one coloured button. Decide it with the style family, and put it in every beat.
- **A beat every 1–2 s, the peak about 3 s** (the hit plus a 30–45f settle; after that, whatever holds is held by the hold formula like any line: a one-word payoff held 85f after the hit read as a stall). An 8 s sting is object → claim → "now live" → URL. At 20–30 s: hook (≤ 2 s), 3–5 claim/proof pairs, the peak on a product beat, the URL card. Longer than 30 s needs acts (problem → reveal → demo), not more pairs.
- **The opener moves from frame 0.** A kinetic opener counts as a hook when it is already in motion on frame 0 and resolves into a glyph or the product within 2 s (letters scatter and re-form around an inline link glyph; a typed request; a word built on an edge glow). A static "Introducing" card is still a template tell.
- **Make the product cinematic, not flat.** Lift the one control that is the feature out of the UI onto a clean ground at poster scale; tilt a real screen in perspective with a rim light and push slowly; let a typed request be the headline. Every product beat gets at least one of a perspective tilt, a rim or horizon light, or a push of ≥ 2× onto the element that carries its claim; a locked camera on a small flat artboard for the whole film is the flat failure. Magnify until every element that carries the argument is ≥ 40 px at 1080p; a whole dense dark screen at feed size is texture, the commonest failure in the collection.
- **Copy rhythm.** Write cards, not sentences: "Now ___" (live, available, yours), a term per card with a full stop ("Fixed terms."), a contrarian opener ("We replaced …"), a setup and payoff split across two cards with the payoff brighter, a pronoun turn ("you" → "YOU", larger and heavier, on its own card), a number with its unit, the URL as the last card. One accent word per line, on the benefit noun, not the brand. At least half the cards say a fact only this product can say: a stack made only of launch clichés ("by design", "unstoppable", "available worldwide") reads as parody, and audiences now laugh at it.
- **Let the glyph into the sentence.** The noun's own icon, coin, photo or a cycling stack of examples sits inline at cap height ("Trade [stack] anything"). It is drawn in the film's material and sized to the type, never a system emoji.
- **Give it a physical payoff.** The best short films end their proof on something you can almost touch: coins thrown out of a toggle, confetti from the amount field, rival icons dropped in a bin, the bet button at poster scale with winnings ticking. A title is not a payoff.

On Three.js the grounds, glows and glossy material are in `three-look` (`references/social-looks.md`), glow-resolve, smear and the rebus slot in `three-type` (`references/reveals.md` §13–15), and tilted UI, card fans and floors in `three-camera` (`references/moves.md` §14–15).

## Building it

The choices are engine-neutral. On **Three.js** (the default) the device usually becomes one persisting object or plane (`three-assets` for UI and textures, `three-camera` for the one big move, `three-transitions` for a carrier), and the restraint budget is easiest to keep with one material and one light (`three-look`). On **HyperFrames** and **React**, the same lines map to sub-compositions or scenes; keep the carrier element shared between them so a handoff is a real continuity, not two similar shapes.

## Good and bad

- Bad: "Introducing" on a dark gradient, a feature-tag cloud, five feature cards, a logo. Good: the product's own output assembles into its mark, which then opens into the first screen.
- Bad: a sync feature explained with devices and arrows. Good: a small interruption makes the viewer need sync, and the same work is simply there on the second screen.
- Bad: every component demo two seconds long under a steady track. Good: the hero component gets twice the time, the minor ones a quick montage, and the music lifts on the hero's reveal.
- Bad: a sound feature announced in silence, or under music. Good: the bed leaves on the first press, and each press is heard alone.
- Bad: a dark, moody reveal with one second of black at the head. Good: something already moving and bright at frame 0, darkness used around it.
- Bad: a 20 s feed film of six glow cards ("Introducing", "Seamless", "By design", "Available now") around one tilted, unreadable dashboard. Good: a kinetic opener that resolves into the feature's own control, three cards that each state a term, each followed by that control acting at poster scale, and a physical payoff before the URL card.
- Bad: an AI builder demo with a marketing line over the editor. Good: the user's request types at headline size, the data source's icon docks into the composer as it is named, and the built result cuts in already moving.

## The taste test

Ask of the cut, on captured frames; any "no" is a note. The full anti-pattern list (what weak launch films do) is in `references/anti-patterns.md`; read it when a film "looks templated" and you cannot say why.

1. Can I say the one sentence after one muted view?
2. Would it lose its meaning as a still?
3. Is the device made of this product's own material, would it break with a competitor's name on it, and is it visible in every proof beat?
4. Is frame 0 in motion, with the tension on screen by 1 s?
5. One peak (or the two-peak shape), built from the idea, with a calmer breath before it, readable with the sound off?
6. One accent used only for meaning, one broken rule at the climax, one transition grammar?
7. Does rhythm follow the story (busy fast, resolve slow, the hero beat longer)?
8. Is each sound on or just after its event, as long as its motion, pointing the right way?
9. Does the bed step aside where the product or the payoff must be heard?
10. Does it end by arriving: a button, a tail, a held lockup that is alive?
11. Is there an element I would delete if I had to cut one? Delete it.
